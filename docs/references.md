# Redesign References

These products are references for craft and interaction quality, not templates to reproduce. The Orbit should keep its own identity as a Hyderabad technology-community guide. The target is a polished, editorial finish—not visual imitation or decorative complexity for its own sake.

## Product references

| Reference | What to study | How The Orbit can apply the principle |
|---|---|---|
| [Linear](https://linear.app/) | Dense information presented with calm hierarchy; consistent surfaces, typography, and interaction states | Make event listings and admin operations feel considered and predictable without making the public pages feel like project-management software |
| [Luma](https://lu.ma/) | Event discovery patterns, image-led event identity, and concise event context | Make each event immediately scannable and inviting while keeping date, attendance mode, and organizer legible |
| [Vercel](https://vercel.com/) | Strong typographic hierarchy, restrained high-contrast branding, and a coherent product/developer voice | Use confident headlines and precise metadata, with a single intentional Orbit accent rather than competing decorative colors |
| [Stripe](https://stripe.com/) | Content-led storytelling, polished transitions, and high clarity around the primary action | Treat event details and registration as a complete, trustworthy journey; keep calls to action obvious and supporting content legible |
| [Raycast](https://www.raycast.com/) | Distinctive product personality, focused surfaces, and purposeful motion | Give the brand one memorable signature detail and keep motion short, useful, and respectful of user preferences |
| [Framer](https://www.framer.com/) | Editorial layouts, expressive but controlled visual rhythm, and strong presentation of visual content | Let real community/event photography create variety while preserving a robust image fallback and responsive composition |

## Reference interpretation

### Common craft principles

- **One clear hierarchy:** establish where to look first, what matters next, and what action is available.
- **Consistency at every state:** loading, empty, unavailable, and error states should feel designed with the same care as the populated screen.
- **Purposeful density:** compact metadata is useful for event discovery; keep it aligned and readable rather than shrinking text.
- **Brand restraint:** use color and motion as signals, not persistent decoration. The Orbit's local and community identity must come from its content and voice.
- **Warm dark-first foundation:** use the approved Orbit Afterdark palette, subtle borders, warm readable text, and one muted accent for deliberate emphasis; avoid cold blue-grey surfaces.
- **Event-first imagery:** use consistent card imagery and composition as the primary source of visual variation. Avoid generic gradients, random illustrations, glassmorphism, and ornamental effects that compete with event artwork.
- **Grid discipline:** event cards must remain two-up on phones and four-up on desktop, with fixed image ratio and consistent card geometry.
- **Restraint:** avoid neon, unnecessary glow, excessive glassmorphism, oversized rounded cards, generic SaaS dashboard aesthetics, and decoration without a clear purpose.
- **Responsive completeness:** navigation, browsing, and event actions should remain available and legible on a phone.
- **Functional polish:** feedback, focus, disabled controls, transitions, and accessible semantics are part of the design—not finishing touches.

### Orbit-specific interpretation

- Keep the weekend-first lens as a recognizable product idea, but make the selected dates and what can be browsed obvious.
- Make Hyderabad and its organizers visible through relevant content and local time, not generic “space” imagery.
- Use organizer identity and event imagery to communicate community while keeping event details structurally consistent.
- Keep the admin quieter and more utilitarian than the public landing experience, but use the same foundations and status language.
- Do not imitate any reference's logos, exact layouts, copy, illustrations, proprietary components, or visual identity.
